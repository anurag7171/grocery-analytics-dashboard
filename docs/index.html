Business Case Study

From Raw Prices to Shopping Decisions

How the project turns 1,275 supermarket products into answers a shopper, category manager, or pricing analyst can act on.

The Question
After the cost-of-living squeeze, which grocery categories are most expensive, where are own-brand savings largest, how often do specials actually run, and where are prices heading next quarter?
Problem framing
The Approach
Collected 1,275 live Woolworths products, stored them in SQL, modelled a 52-week price history, ran statistical tests (Welch's t-test, Spearman correlation, OLS regression) and a Prophet forecast, then surfaced it all through Tableau and an interactive app.
Method
The Findings
National brands cost up to 130% more than own-brand; cheaper items receive the deepest discounts; Dairy shows the steepest implied inflation (4.7%/yr); Snacks and Drinks go on special roughly every five weeks.
Insight
The Recommendation
Switch to own-brand in Snacks and Drinks for the largest savings, buy Dairy ahead of its rising trend, and time purchases to the five-week promo cycle in the most-discounted categories.

Key Insights from the Data

Statistical analysis across 1,275 products revealing real pricing patterns in the Australian grocery market.

Snacks: 130% National Brand Premium
Welch's t-test confirms national brands cost 130.5% more than own-brand equivalents in the Snacks category — the largest premium of any department (p < 0.001, large effect size).
p < 0.001 Cohen's d = large
Cheaper Products Get Deeper Discounts
Spearman correlation shows a strong negative relationship between price and discount depth in Bakery (ρ = -0.78) and Dairy (ρ = -0.83) — budget items are promoted more aggressively.
Spearman ρ = -0.83
Dairy Running Highest Implied Inflation
OLS regression on 52-week price trends reveals Dairy & Eggs tracking at 4.71% implied annual inflation — the steepest of all 10 categories and well above RBA's 2–3% target band.
4.71% / year
Fruit & Veg: Most Volatile Prices
Fruit & Veg shows extreme right-skewed price distribution (skewness 4.2, kurtosis 28.8) — a handful of premium items like truffles and specialty mushrooms pull the tail hard right.
Skewness 4.2
Snacks & Drinks: Most Promotional Activity
Snacks and Drinks products go on special ~11 times per year — every 4.7 weeks on average. Household and Health & Wellness run promotions least frequently (~7–8 weeks apart).
11× / year
Budget Tier Gets Largest Specials
Percentile-based price tier segmentation shows Budget products (avg $2.88) receive an average 5.1% discount when on special vs 3.3% for Premium products — promotional strategy targets the value-conscious shopper.
Budget avg $2.88

SQL Showcase

Business Queries

12 production-ready SQL queries answering real business questions about pricing, promotions, and value.

Brand vs Own Brand — Avg Price by Category
SELECT
    category,
    CASE
        WHEN LOWER(COALESCE(brand,'')) LIKE '%woolworths%'
          OR  brand IS NULL
        THEN 'Own Brand'
        ELSE 'National Brand'
    END                          AS brand_type,
    COUNT(*)                     AS product_count,
    ROUND(AVG(price), 2)         AS avg_price,
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(is_on_special)
          / COUNT(*), 1)          AS special_pct
FROM   products
WHERE  scraped_at = (SELECT MAX(scraped_at) FROM products)
GROUP  BY category, brand_type
ORDER  BY category, brand_type;
Top Discounted Products Right Now
SELECT
    name,
    brand,
    category,
    price,
    was_price,
    discount_pct,
    unit_size
FROM   products
WHERE  is_on_special = 1
  AND  scraped_at = (SELECT MAX(scraped_at) FROM products)
ORDER  BY discount_pct DESC
LIMIT  25;
Price Volatility — Most Unpredictable Products
SELECT
    p.name,
    p.category,
    ROUND(h.avg_price, 2)          AS hist_avg,
    ROUND(h.price_std, 2)          AS std_dev,
    ROUND(100.0 * h.price_std
          / h.avg_price, 1)         AS cv_pct
FROM   products p
JOIN (
    SELECT product_id,
           AVG(price)   AS avg_price,
           SQRT(AVG(price*price)
                - AVG(price)*AVG(price))
                        AS price_std
    FROM   price_history_weekly
    GROUP  BY product_id
) h ON p.product_id = h.product_id
ORDER  BY cv_pct DESC
LIMIT  20;
Weekly Promotional Heatmap Data
SELECT
    week_date,
    category,
    ROUND(
        100.0 * SUM(is_on_special)
               / COUNT(*), 1
    )                        AS special_pct,
    COUNT(*)                 AS products_tracked
FROM   price_history_weekly
GROUP  BY week_date, category
ORDER  BY week_date, category;
